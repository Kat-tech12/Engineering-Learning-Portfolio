Writing that is Clear, Concise, Cohesive, Coherent, and Appropriate for the Audience

The ability to write concisely and clearly is an important skill that is necessary in many professions. The organization and clarity of that writing is crucial in determining how clear the writing is. When I was writing a portion of the Project 2 Final Paper, it was noted that there was not a lot of clarity in my writing and the organization of it was not done very well (A-1). What I had written in that paper had very little flow and was a large block of text, which can be hard to read and make sense of within the paper. Since then, I have learned how to organize a paper or paragraph better to make it more readable. Similarly, my writing is much clearer and does not stand out from the rest of the paper as well. This change is shown in the Project 3 Final Paper, where my writing is clearer and much more concise than in the previous example (A-2).

Presentations Delivered Confidently with the Appropriate Information, Audience Level, and Technique

Good presentation practices and confidence are an effective way to demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the topic, communication, and confidence. An example of bad presentation practices would be Reading 7 Effective Presentations, where I learned about good presentation practices and then built a small presentation that included the exact opposite (A-3). By doing this, I learned what good practices look like and how effective they are, along with just how detrimental the bad practices are. It also included how communication with the audience can essentially make or break the presentation. Good communication and speaking can show confidence and knowledge. The communication technique that stood out the most to me was the tone of the speaker. Using a light and clear tone works better with most audiences rather than an aggressive or condescending tone, which is also noted in Reading 7 (A-3).

Application to Professional Practice

Concise and clear writing along with good presentation and communication can be used in an engineering profession. I can use both skills to write reports and presentations to get information out to my coworkers and the community. Furthering my progress in my writing skills will make technology reports and general reports cleaner and easier to read. The presentation and speaking techniques I have learned can make my presentations easy to understand and make me sound more confident.

Engage in Dynamic, Shared Leadership

When working in a team, sometimes it is important for someone to be leading the team to help guide the other members and help with problems when necessary. That person is not always the one leading in some scenarios. A lot of the time the role of the leader shifts depending on each member’s proficiency in the current task. In the team I was a part of, we split responsibilities based on everyone’s skills and availability. The person who was most proficient in a specific task would take the lead that day and would help other members understand the task as well as they did and would resolve any problems. These strengths and weaknesses were determined from an assignment we did in class called Setting Goals and Standards with your Team (B-1). What was surprising about this was how every member was proficient in something different and between the whole team there were not that many gaps that needed to be bridged, which made working together much easier.

Demonstrate Interdependence and Accountablilty

At the very beginning of working with my team, it was a little hard to find a balance of working together. It felt like we were not truly working together as a group and holding each other accountable. A technique we were introduced to during this class to solve this was creating a team contract, which solved most of our issues (B-3). This contract gave us an outline of expectations and what to do if they are not met. After each project, we would look at this contract and decide as a team if any revisions were needed. However, we still had problems with procrastination and not getting work done in time for others to finish their parts or proof-read important papers. To solve this, we were introduced to a Project Plan that helped organize assignments and due dates. This tactic worked so well that we used it for both Project 2 and Project 3 (B-4), (B-5).

Application to Professional Practice

The ability to effectively work in a team is a very important skill to have as an engineer. By understanding dynamic leadership and accountability, teams can be efficient and enjoy working with each other. I plan on using the techniques shown to me to further my teamwork skills which will allow me to comfortably work in a team in the professional world.

Identifying Relevant Stakeholders and their Needs, Interests, and/or Concerns

Engineering decisions must consider many factors and one of those factors is the stakeholders and their needs, interests, and concerns. Stakeholders are typically the people who will be affected the most by these decisions, so being able to identify them is an important skill to have. In the past, I did not fully understand how exactly someone could be a stakeholder in a decision or problem. The Project 3 Stakeholder Analysis helped me understand just how many people can be affected by a problem and its possible solutions (C-1). In the stakeholder analysis we documented stakeholders and how the problem we had would affect them along with their concerns about implementing a possible solution. By doing this, my team was taught to consider all possibilities and how our technology might meet stakeholders’ needs.

Collecting and Analyzing Potentially Conflicting Data and Viewpoints

Another important thing my team learned was that not everyone has the same ideals or viewpoint on a problem or topic. A solution that might work for one group of people might not work for another, or they might not like the implementation of it. To help understand this, we were introduced to ethical lenses in Reading 13 Markkula Center Ethical Frameworks and Toolkit (C-2). This reading gave us some insight into how to collect this conflicting data and analyze why it was conflicted. The technique of using lenses has proven useful in all of Project 3, where we had to make an ethical decision about our technology while considering stakeholders and their needs and concerns (C-3).

Application to Professional Practice

In an engineering profession, I can use both the skill of identifying a stakeholder and their needs along with using ethical lenses to analyze conflicting viewpoints to get a fuller understanding of a problem or how I might go about designing a solution. By doing this, I have a view of the problem from the perspective of the stakeholders, and I can design a solution that works for more people and fits the ideals of the stakeholders that it would affect.
